Two missions. Both short. Neither one lets you write anything useful yet, which is
the point.

A Forward Deployed Engineer starts every project in the same position. You know less
about the business than everyone in the room. You know less about the codebase than
the engineer sitting next to you. The only thing you have that they do not is
distance. You are the one person who has not spent four years working around the
broken parts, so you are the one person who can still see them.

That distance has a shelf life. Two weeks in, you will have absorbed the same
assumptions everyone else has. Whatever you notice in week one is worth writing down
immediately, even the things that seem too obvious to mention.

## What you do here

Mission 01 sets up how the engagement works. Who hired you, what they think they
bought, and what your actual job is. Jordan from sales has already promised something.
You find out what.

Mission 02 boots the Northstar system on your laptop and walks you through it. Four
Java services, a Python service that barely exists yet, Postgres, Kafka, Redis, and
four vendor integrations that are all pretending to work. You will not understand it.
You are not supposed to yet.

## What you will get wrong

Almost everyone reads the CEO's email and starts designing an underwriting model in
their head. That instinct is normal and it is expensive. Hold it loosely for the next
six missions.
