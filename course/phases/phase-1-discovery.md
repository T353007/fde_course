Five missions, and no code in any of them.

This is the part new FDEs skip. They skip it because it feels like it is not
engineering, and because there is real pressure to show progress. Then they build the
wrong thing well, which is the most expensive outcome available.

Northstar's CEO wants processing time down 70 percent, and he wants it done with an
AI underwriter. Those are two separate claims. One of them is a goal and one of them
is a solution, and he has fused them into a single sentence. Your first job is to pull
them apart without making him feel corrected.

## What you do here

You interview eleven people. They will not agree. The underwriter and the product
manager will describe processes that share almost no steps. The engineer will tell you
about a function nobody else knows exists. Support will casually mention a workaround
that turns out to explain a third of the delay.

Then you build a real workflow map with real numbers on it, and the numbers do
something uncomfortable. They show that the thing you were hired to build cannot
produce the outcome you were hired to produce.

Mission 06 is the conversation. Mission 07 is what you propose instead.

## What you will get wrong

Two things, usually.

First, you will treat the loudest stakeholder as the most informed one. In this
building that is Marcus, and he is confidently wrong about where time goes.

Second, you will believe the timestamps in the database. They are accurate and they
are not measuring what you think they measure. Mission 05 is where that catches up
with you.
